# Cyclistic Case Study Findings

## Rider Type Comparison

### Query Objective

Compare ride volume and average ride duration between casual riders and annual members.

### Results

Members had a higher total number of rides (**3,564,541**) than casual riders (**2,064,263**) but had a lower average ride length.

- **Members:** 12.30 minutes
- **Casual riders:** 24.11 minutes

### Insight

Annual members generated approximately **63% of all rides**, indicating more frequent use of the bike-share system.

Casual riders averaged **24.11 minutes per ride** compared to **12.30 minutes for members**. Casual riders spend nearly twice as long on each ride.

This suggests that casual riders are more likely to use bikes for leisure or occasional trips, while annual members may use the service for routine transportation and commuting.

## Weekday Usage Analysis

### Query Objective

Determine whether annual members and casual riders use Cyclistic bikes differently throughout the week.

### Results

Annual members generated the highest number of rides during the workweek, peaking on **Wednesday with 565,906 rides** and Thursday with **555,267 rides**. Member ride volume declined on the weekend, especially on Sunday.

Casual riders showed the opposite pattern. Their ride volume increased toward the weekend, with **Saturday recording the highest number of casual rides at 425,623**.

### Insight

The weekday pattern suggests that annual members use Cyclistic more consistently for routine transportation, such as commuting, errands, or regular daily travel.

Casual riders appear more active during the weekend, which suggests recreational, leisure, or tourism-related usage.

This supports the earlier finding that casual riders take longer trips on average than annual members. Together, the ride count and ride duration patterns suggest that casual riders behave more like leisure users, while members behave more like routine transportation users.

## Monthly Usage Analysis

### Query Objective

Determine how ride volume changes throughout the year for annual members and casual riders.

### Results

Both rider groups experienced increased usage during warmer months and decreased usage during winter months. Ride volume peaked in **September** for both groups, with **474,373 rides from annual members** and **346,494 rides from casual riders**.

Annual members maintained relatively consistent usage throughout the year, even during winter months. Casual riders displayed much stronger seasonality, with ride volume increasing substantially during the summer and early fall before declining sharply during winter.

### Insight

The strong seasonal pattern among casual riders suggests that weather and outdoor recreational activities play a significant role in their usage behavior.

Annual members appear less affected by seasonality and continue using the service throughout the year, supporting the idea that members rely on Cyclistic bikes as a routine transportation option.

The combination of seasonal usage, weekend riding patterns, and longer trip durations indicates that casual riders are more likely to use the service for leisure and recreational purposes rather than daily transportation.

## Bike Type Preference Analysis

### Query Objective

Compare bike type usage between annual members and casual riders to identify rider preferences and differences in equipment utilization.

### Results

Electric bikes were the most popular vehicle type for both rider groups, followed by classic bikes and electric scooters.

Members recorded **1,967,397 electric bike rides** and **1,538,022 classic bike rides**. Casual riders recorded **1,103,948 electric bike rides** and **875,100 classic bike rides**.

Electric scooters represented a relatively small share of total rides but were used proportionally more often by casual riders than annual members.

### Insight

The popularity of electric bikes among both rider groups suggests that convenience and accessibility are important factors in vehicle selection.

The higher proportional use of electric scooters among casual riders may indicate greater interest in recreational or exploratory trips. This finding further supports earlier evidence that casual riders behave differently from annual members and may represent a valuable target segment for membership conversion campaigns.

## Final Recommendations

### Recommendation 1: Target Weekend Riders

Casual riders demonstrate significantly higher ride volume and longer ride durations on weekends, particularly on Saturdays and Sundays.

Cyclistic should promote weekend-focused membership campaigns, such as:

- Weekend membership discounts
- Seasonal membership promotions
- Free trial memberships for frequent weekend riders

These campaigns should be targeted toward riders who repeatedly use the service on weekends.

### Recommendation 2: Promote Cost Savings for Frequent Casual Riders

Although casual riders take fewer trips overall, their average ride duration is nearly twice that of annual members.

Cyclistic should communicate the potential cost savings of membership by showing casual riders how much they could save over time compared to single-ride pricing.

### Recommendation 3: Focus Marketing During Peak Riding Season

Casual rider usage increases dramatically during summer and early fall months, peaking in September.

Cyclistic should concentrate membership marketing efforts between **May and September** when casual rider activity is highest and riders are most engaged with the platform.

## Conclusion

Analysis revealed clear behavioral differences between annual members and casual riders. Members use Cyclistic more frequently and consistently throughout the week and year, suggesting transportation-focused usage. Casual riders demonstrate stronger weekend activity, longer ride durations, and greater seasonality, indicating recreational and leisure-oriented usage.

The findings suggest that targeting active casual riders with weekend-focused promotions, membership cost-saving messaging, and seasonal marketing campaigns may increase conversion from casual riders to annual members.
