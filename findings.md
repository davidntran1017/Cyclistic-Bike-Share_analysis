\# Cyclistic Case Study Findings



\## Rider Type Comparison



\### Query Objective



Compare ride volume and average ride duration between casual riders and annual members.



\### Results



Members had a higher total number of rides (\*\*3,564,541\*\*) than casual riders (\*\*2,064,263\*\*) but had a lower average ride length.



\- \*\*Members:\*\* 12.30 minutes

\- \*\*Casual riders:\*\* 24.11 minutes



\### Insight



Annual members generated approximately \*\*63% of all rides\*\*, indicating more frequent use of the bike-share system.



Casual riders averaged \*\*24.11 minutes per ride\*\* compared to \*\*12.30 minutes for members\*\*. Casual riders spend nearly twice as long on each ride.



This suggests that casual riders are more likely to use bikes for leisure or occasional trips, while annual members may use the service for routine transportation and commuting.





\## Weekday Usage Analysis



\### Query Objective



Determine whether annual members and casual riders use Cyclistic bikes differently throughout the week.



\### Results



Annual members generated the highest number of rides during the workweek, peaking on \*\*Wednesday with 565,906 rides\*\* and Thursday with \*\*555,267 rides\*\*. Member ride volume declined on the weekend, especially on Sunday.



Casual riders showed the opposite pattern. Their ride volume increased toward the weekend, with \*\*Saturday recording the highest number of casual rides at 425,623\*\*.



Casual riders also took longer rides throughout the week, with average ride duration increasing to \*\*26.89 minutes on Saturday\*\* and \*\*28.20 minutes on Sunday\*\*.



\### Insight



The weekday pattern suggests that annual members use Cyclistic more consistently for routine transportation, such as commuting, errands, or regular daily travel.



Casual riders appear more active during the weekend, which suggests recreational, leisure, or tourism-related usage.



This supports the earlier finding that casual riders take longer trips on average than annual members. Together, the ride count and ride duration patterns suggest that casual riders behave more like leisure users, while members behave more like routine transportation users.





\## Monthly Usage Analysis



\### Query Objective



Determine how ride volume changes throughout the year for annual members and casual riders.



\### Results



Both rider groups experienced increased usage during warmer months and decreased usage during winter months. Ride volume peaked in \*\*September\*\* for both groups, with \*\*474,373 rides from annual members\*\* and \*\*346,494 rides from casual riders\*\*.



Annual members maintained relatively consistent usage throughout the year, even during winter months. Casual riders displayed much stronger seasonality, with ride volume increasing substantially during the summer and early fall before declining sharply during winter.



\### Insight



The strong seasonal pattern among casual riders suggests that weather and outdoor recreational activities may play a role in their usage behavior.



Annual members appear less affected by seasonality and continue using the service throughout the year, supporting the idea that members may rely on Cyclistic bikes more consistently for routine transportation.



The combination of seasonal usage, weekend riding patterns, and longer trip durations suggests that casual riders are more likely to use the service for leisure and recreational purposes rather than daily transportation.





\## Bike Type Preference Analysis



\### Query Objective



Compare bike type usage between annual members and casual riders to identify rider preferences and differences in equipment utilization.



\### Results



Electric bikes were the most popular vehicle type for both rider groups, followed by classic bikes and electric scooters.



\- \*\*Members:\*\* 1,967,397 electric bike rides and 1,538,022 classic bike rides

\- \*\*Casual riders:\*\* 1,103,948 electric bike rides and 875,100 classic bike rides



Electric scooters represented a relatively small share of total rides but were used proportionally more often by casual riders than annual members.



\### Insight



The popularity of electric bikes among both rider groups suggests that electric bikes could be an effective focus for marketing efforts aimed at both groups.



The higher proportional use of electric scooters among casual riders provides another example of differences in vehicle usage between casual riders and annual members.





\# Final Recommendations



\## Recommendation 1: Target Weekend Riders



Casual riders demonstrate higher ride volume and longer ride durations on weekends, particularly on Saturdays and Sundays.



Cyclistic could focus membership promotions on periods when casual riders are most active, including:



\- Weekend-focused membership promotions

\- Seasonal membership promotions

\- Trial or introductory membership offers for frequent casual riders



These campaigns could be targeted toward riders who repeatedly use the service on weekends.





\## Recommendation 2: Communicate Membership Value to Frequent Casual Riders



Casual riders take substantially longer trips than annual members on average.



Cyclistic could target frequent casual riders with messaging that communicates the potential value and benefits of becoming an annual member.



Further analysis of Cyclistic's pricing structure and individual rider behavior would be needed to quantify specific cost savings.





\## Recommendation 3: Focus Marketing During Peak Riding Season



Casual rider usage increases substantially during summer and early fall, with ride volume peaking in September.



Cyclistic could concentrate membership marketing efforts during the warmer months when casual rider activity is highest and riders are most engaged with the service.





\# Conclusion



The analysis revealed clear behavioral differences between annual members and casual riders. Members use Cyclistic more frequently and consistently throughout the week and year, suggesting more routine transportation-oriented usage. Casual riders demonstrate stronger weekend activity, longer ride durations, and greater seasonality, suggesting more recreational and leisure-oriented usage.



These findings suggest that targeting active casual riders with weekend-focused promotions, membership-value messaging, and seasonal marketing campaigns may provide opportunities to increase conversion from casual riders to annual members.
